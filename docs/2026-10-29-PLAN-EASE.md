# Build planner: ease of assembly on the meshes

Kind: record of a change. Implements the five steps proposed in
[PLAN-REVIEW](2026-10-29-PLAN-REVIEW.md). The working reference is
[PLAN](2026-09-25-PLAN.md), updated alongside ("Joints and how parts go
in", "What it checks", the cost table and "Limits").

## Summary

`coscad plan` now measures every assembly on its native meshes before it
searches. It checks how each part gets in, not only where it ends up. It
knows how printed joints are made: pushed, dropped into an open socket, or
screwed together. It counts the hands each move needs and the alignment
work of each joint, and it orders parts so that none buries another's last
way in. Each step reports its cost terms, and `coscad calibrate` fits the
weights to timed builds.

Tesseract III now plans from its joints alone: the cap `after=` hints and
the generated `fastener peg` lines are gone. The old bolted tesseract is
now reported as unbuildable from rigid parts. Its earlier box-based plan
finished by placing the inner cube inside the closed frame.

## The five steps

### 1. Joint motion

`joint push|drop|screw moving host [pitch=mm] [axis=face]` declares how a
joint without hardware is made. The axis comes from the mate between the
two parts (the host's frame normal), so the line adds no geometry. With
`axis=`, a world face of the host stands in for `asm`-placed assemblies.
The moving part goes on after its host.

- `push`: a press or snap fit; it holds once seated.
- `drop`: an open socket; it holds only by gravity or capture.
- `screw`: an integral thread; `pitch=` is required and the moving part
  turns.

### 2. Motion checked against the meshes

Every part's ways in are swept against every other part at its final pose.
The candidate directions are the 26 directions of a cube lattice, both
directions of each joint axis, each mated frame normal and each of the
part's own axes, and the combined way out of two or three joints. The
lattice is what finds Tesseract II's bars, which enter at 45° to the frame.

- **Sweeps.** A sweep advances conservatively (a part g mm from another can
  move g mm). Where two parts touch, a copy of the moving part shrunk by
  `contact` tells touching from pushing in. A blocked way is closed; a way
  with no fingertip room behind the part is closed too.
- **Alignment.** Seating a part against a joint partner costs its Fitts
  index, log2(1 + D/W): D the engaged length, W the play at the seat,
  measured by shifting the part sideways until it meets the partner.
- **Screw joints.** Turns are the engaged length over the pitch. The moving
  part is spun about its axis at the seat against every other part, and two
  fingertips need room around it.
- **Order from burial.** A part that alone closes every way in that another
  part still has (after its certain predecessors) must come after it. It is
  derived before the search and repeated to a fixed point; a part left with
  no way in is a design error.
- **Dead ends.** The search drops any state in which a part still to place
  has lost its last way in.

### 3. Real geometry

- **Mass and centre of mass.** Taken from the mesh. A printed part is its
  perimeter shell (`shell` mm times its surface area) plus `infill` of the
  rest; metal is solid. `mass=` still wins.
- **Bench footprint.** The hull of each part's lowest vertices.
- **Resting on parts.** The contact patch is the kernel overlap of the part,
  lowered by `settle`, with the part under it. A part in a seat with print
  clearance comes to rest when it drops that far.
- **Rest faces.** When the assembly is not square to the world, the largest
  part's own faces are added. A tilted assembly rests on its base part's
  bottom face, not on an edge.

### 4. Holding and hands

After each move, a placed part stays put if it is on the bench, or
(repeatedly) joined by a made joint that holds, resting balanced on, or
captured by parts already known to stay. Every other piece needs a hand,
with parts joined together counting once.

- One hand does each move. Each hand beyond two costs `hand` (20) and the
  step says so ("This needs another pair of hands (or a jig)").
- During a flip, gravity holds nothing. Pieces not joined to or captured by
  the heaviest piece must be held, and the two hands turning the assembly
  can press one each.
- A rail or bar standing on end is only flagged while nothing braces it.
- The completion estimate charges a coming flip for the loose pieces it
  will have to hold. The phase search counts that in a move's marginal
  cost, so it stops dropping in bars that a later flip would shake loose.

### 5. Calibration

- **Settings.** Every threshold behind the old weights is now a `plan`
  setting with a documented default: `driver_angle`, `standing_angle`,
  `slender`, `driver_length`, `driver_margin`, `phase_tau`, `step_parts`,
  `finger_size`, `contact`, `settle`, `sweep_step`, `shell` and `infill`.
- **Terms.** Each step's terms (`flip`, `vertical`, `driver`, `shrink`,
  `sibling`, `loose`, `insert`, `turn`, `hand`, `finger`, `part`,
  `fastener`, `nut`) and their weighted cost are in the plan JSON.
- **Fitting.** `coscad calibrate times.json` reads plan JSON files and the
  seconds each step took. It fits non-negative weights by least squares
  (Lawson and Hanson) and prints them as a `plan` line in seconds, with R²
  and the typical step error. Terms no timed step used keep their defaults.

```json
{ "builds": [ { "plan": "tesseract_plan.json", "step_seconds": [40, 35, null, 90] } ] }
```

`null` marks a step that was not timed. No real build has been timed yet,
so the defaults are still guesses: time a build step by step (a phone
stopwatch per step is enough) and run `calibrate`.

## Speed

The geometry stage is a few hundred thousand distance queries. Three
changes made it usable:

- **Own distance query.** `Manifold::MinGap` runs a full Boolean
  intersection on every call. The bridge now keeps its own bounding-volume
  tree of each posed solid and searches two trees best-first, with a
  triangle-box prefilter.
- **Shrunken copy.** Touching is told from overlapping with the shrunken
  copy instead of a Boolean.
- **Parallel.** Parts are measured on all cores, with the collector limited
  to four threads (`-with-rtsopts=-N -qn4`, set for the executable and the
  test suite).

The search also got faster, by carrying each state's loose pieces and
precomputing joint partners and approach blockers.

## Results

Local, macOS arm64 (10 cores), every plannable assembly on the manual and
in the examples. "Before" is main at e89b77e (boxes); costs are not
comparable across the two (new terms), so the table shows steps and flips.

| Assembly | Before | After | Time before → after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| corner pair | 3 steps, 0 flips | 3 steps, 0 flips | 0.7 s → 0.1 s |
| rail corner | 4 steps, 1 flip | 5 steps, 0 flips | 0.05 s → 2 s |
| bolted ball | 3 steps, 2 flips | 3 steps, 2 flips | 0.01 s → 0.3 s |
| toy bow | 3 steps, 0 flips | 3 steps, 0 flips | 0.04 s → 1 s |
| Orbit Tower | 19 steps, 0 flips | 19 steps, 0 flips | 0.05 s → 0.5 s |
| 2020 cube | 28 steps, 6 flips | 29 steps, 6 flips | 18 s → 24 s |
| Tesseract II | 36 steps, 0 flips | 36 steps, 2 flips | 11 s → 11 s |
| bolted tesseract | 28 steps, 1 flip | refused: no rigid order | 6.5 s → 5 s |
| Tesseract III | 36 steps, 2 flips (generated pegs and cap hints) | 36 steps, 5 flips, 4 extra hands (joints only) | 127 s → 138 s |

- Tesseract II's 0-flip plan brought the bottom bars in from below,
  through the bench; with the frame upright they can only enter that way.
  The new plan turns the frame twice. Its file still carries its `after=`
  hints; they now agree with the derived order.
- Tesseract III plans with no hints. Every hub goes on before its bars,
  every cap after all three bars it captures (derived: the cap would bury
  their only way in). Every threaded joint takes about 2.5 turns (8 mm at
  3 mm pitch measures 7.5 mm engaged). The extra hands are bars resting in
  open sockets while the frame is turned to reach the next diagonal. The
  default weights trade 20 per hand against 10 per flip; calibration
  decides that.
- `coscad calibrate` on Tesseract III's plan with step times generated
  from known weights (one step left untimed) recovers them to two decimals
  (R² 1.000).

Test suite: 1659 checks pass with the OpenSCAD oracle installed (1646 on
main). New: a peg in a hole (log2(1 + 16/0.4) bits, lowered from above),
a lid that must follow the peg it covers, a part sealed in its host
(design error), a knobbed screw joint (8 turns) and a flush one (cannot be
gripped), a T-handle whose rotation orders a post after it, mesh mass,
setting and joint-line validation, calibration recovering known weights,
and Tesseract III's order and turns.

## Limits

- **Rigid parts.** A design that only closes by flexing is unbuildable to
  the planner.
- **Straight paths.** Ways in are straight lines in the candidate
  directions. A part that must follow a bent path, or rotate as it goes in,
  is not found.
- **Screw joints.** Rotation is checked at the seat only. The thread
  against its own partner is not swept; a coupon verifies the fit.
- **Drivers and nuts.** Driver and nut access still use boxes.
- **Burial is pairwise.** A part blocked only by two others together is
  caught by the dead-end check during the search, not ordered in advance.
- **The hand model is simple.** One hand per move, one per loose piece, two
  turning the assembly; no jigs are inferred.
- **Uncalibrated.** The default weights have not been fitted to any timed
  build.
