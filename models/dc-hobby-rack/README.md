# DC hobby motor rack drive

The four-part plate uses the SG90 revision 4 rack tooth profile, rail and pinned keeper. The motor socket assumes a centered round shaft on the 19.7 × 14.8 mm face and a 29.4 mm case length excluding the shaft. Two friction ribs grip the case. The front lip has a 12 mm opening for a motor bearing boss. Check the actual boss and shaft position before printing.

Insert the motor from below until its front face touches the lip, with the leads toward the open side. Support the shaft from behind if accessible while pressing the pinion on, chamfer first. Leave 0.4 mm between pinion and socket roof. The nominal 1.70 mm bore is a trial fit for the smooth shaft. At this position even a 6.7 mm shaft spans the full 4.8 mm gear face.

Place the rack over the rail, teeth toward the shaft, and press the keeper onto the two studs. Start near mid-stroke. The closed 47 mm slot around the 22 mm rail gives 25 mm total travel. The 22-tooth module 1.5 pinion advances the rack about 104 mm per turn. Jog the motor briefly with a current-limited supply or turn the gear by hand for the first check. An end stop stalls a powered DC motor; the example has no automatic cutoff.

## Revision 2 physical result

The owner confirmed the revised motor body and enlarged pinion bore worked. Install the alligator clips upside down on the motor terminals to give the clips enough room in the wiring bay.
