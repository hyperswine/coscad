# Two small floating boats, 2026-10-25

Requested by the owner after the successful threaded globe. All geometry is
CoScad, generated by design.py. Neither boat has yet been physically float-tested.

| Boat | Printer | Size, mm | Slice | PLA | Intended spool |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Conventional open monohull | P1S | 116 x 54 x 28 | 2 h 43 min, 175 layers | 44.78 g | Loaded yellow external PLA |
| Low central hull with two sealed outriggers | A1 | 110 x 98 x 22 | 3 h 58 min, 137 layers | 50.90 g | New white PLA, confirmed physical AMS slot 1 (ams0) |

The conventional boat has a pointed bow, rounded transom, thick low keel and
flared sides. The second is a low, wide outrigger design with a central open hull
and two sealed air chambers, joined by arched crossarms. Its outer form is like
a catamaran, with an extra small central hull. Enclosed pontoon roofs slope
inward so they print without supports trapped inside. Actual bridge segments
in the crossarms are at most about 7.56 mm long.

## Checks and limits

Fully rendered with OpenSCAD Manifold. Each boat has one connected positive
plastic boundary. The outrigger also has two correctly oriented enclosed void
boundaries for its air chambers. Meshes are watertight; this does not establish
that printed layer seams will not leak.

2026-10-29: render.py now renders with the native compiler (`coscad stl`), not
OpenSCAD. The recorded meshes, `*-render.log` files and results above came from
OpenSCAD Manifold. A native re-render matches all six meshes in volume (largest
relative difference 3e-11) and bounds, with slightly more triangles (monohull
1614 against 1608, outrigger 6124 against 6046), and verify.py on those meshes
reproduces validation.json to within 1e-6 mm.

verify.py computes static freshwater displacement and transverse righting arms
for homogeneous solid PLA at 1.24 g/cm3. At sampled angles, the conventional
hull retains an unflooded restoring tendency through 30 degrees; the outrigger
through 35 degrees. This is a fixed-trim static calculation, not guaranteed
self-righting after full capsize or a wave/payload/dynamic analysis. The actual
boats need a shallow-water leak and gentle heel test after cooling.

Both specific printer profiles use a 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.16 mm layers, six requested
walls, six top/bottom layers, 100% zig-zag fill in the modeled plastic, Textured
PEI and a 3 mm outer brim. Supports are disabled. Ordinary slicer paths,
including narrow features and bridging, determine the effective local wall
count. The saved Bambu Studio presets retain conservative PLA temperatures and
speeds from successful earlier prints. Colours are actual spool choices, not
multiple tool changes.

The slicer reported Success and no plate warnings for both. The installed Bambu
Studio 02.08.02.61 uses the canonical infill enum zig-zag, not rectilinear; the
initial rejected settings attempt was corrected before slicing. The emitted
G-code and final settings are checked, including P1S (0,2) and A1 (0,0) extruder
offsets. No support paths are deposited. Estimated slice weights agree with
solid mesh mass within 1%. CLI output omits printer_model_id; verify_packages.py
fills C12/N2S from installed vendor profiles without changing G-code bytes and
checks the ZIP and G-code MD5/SHA-256.

## Files and rebuilding

- design.py, render.py, verify.py: CoScad generation, full meshes, buoyancy checks.
- monohull.coscad / outrigger.coscad: actual printable source; envelope/opening
  sources are numerical helpers, not extra parts to print.
- preview-and-stability.png and validation.json: geometry and numerical results.
- prepare_slice.py / slice.py: editable per-printer 3MF projects and CLI slices.
- verify_packages.py / packages.json: actual toolpath and final package checks.
- monohull/slice/monohull.gcode.3mf: final P1S job.
- outrigger/slice/outrigger.gcode.3mf: final A1 job.

Run these scripts in the listed order with an environment containing trimesh,
manifold3d, numpy, scipy, matplotlib, Pillow and paho-mqtt. CoScad is resolved
through cabal list-bin exe:coscad (render.py also takes `--coscad PATH` or
`$COSCAD`, and `--out DIR`). Bambu Studio may need host access on macOS; since
2026-10-29 no script here needs OpenSCAD. No build/verify script dispatches a print.

printer-context.py reads the explicitly authorized existing .env credentials

names and the dashboard's public CA at /private/tmp/boat-printer-ca.pem. Retrieve

dispatch.py requires a named boat, its exact spool, an explicit bed-clear flag
and an authorization note. It checks immutable package identity and idle/fault
state, writes a durable exclusive record, verifies uploaded size and full remote
SHA-256, then records intent before exactly one MQTT project_file send on the
existing status API client. A missing acknowledgement is reconciled read-only,
never automatically resent. Any existing record blocks another invocation.
The bounded startup check stops after the first layer or an issue; no recurring
monitor is created. test_dispatch.py simulates acknowledged start, lost reply,
corrupt remote content and duplicate invocation without contacting a printer.

The following commands document the completed dispatch. Both now have durable
records and must not be repeated to start another print:

python dispatch.py outrigger --spool ams0 --confirm-bed-clear --authorization-note 'Owner confirmed A1 plate clear and new white PLA in physical slot 1'

## Physical outcome, 2026-10-25 (owner report)

The monohull from the P1S came out decently. In a sink it floated, was quite stable, did not
collapse or list, and its waterline looked a bit lower than halfway up the hull, though the
viewing angle made that hard to judge. verify.py predicted a 15.3 mm draft, 55% of the 28 mm
height, so the real boat sits at or a little above the prediction's level of buoyancy. The
owner's photo is `20261025-monohull-sink-float-owner-photo.jpg` and the record is
`physical-outcome-monohull-20261025.json`. The hull in the photo is white, not the yellow spool
recorded at dispatch. Not yet reported: leaks over time, payload to the rim, heel behaviour,
and the A1 outrigger's result.

The A1 outrigger also printed well and floated stably, with the waterline around the
horizontal line on the hull (predicted draft 10.4 mm, 47% of the 22 mm height). With the
centre hull deliberately filled with water it still floated, as predicted from the two sealed
pontoons alone (58 g of buoyancy for a 51 g boat), and it also floated upside down. First
observations only; see `physical-outcome-outrigger-20261025.json`. The owner's three photos
are in the Photos library (2026-10-25 21:16) and could not be read from the shell; export
them here to keep them with the record.
