# Hand-cranked bath monohull

The 116 × 54 mm hull comes from the twin-boat trials. The 25 mm crank drives a 16:16 straight miter pair; one crank revolution turns the 50 mm three-blade propeller once. The two axes intersect at 90°. The stern shaft slopes down 20° to bring the propeller below the transom. This is a hand-held or steady-by-hand drive for calm shallow water. The original hull floated in a sink and sank when swamped in surf. The propulsion version has not been float-tested.

## Parts

The plate includes the modified hull, detachable bridge, crank spindle gear, keyed crank, shaft gear, upright stern tube and modified P3-50 propeller. Use an approximately 88 mm long smooth 4 mm stainless shaft, two 4 mm ID × 6 mm OD × 6 mm plain bushings, two M5 × 16 screws for the bridge, and three M3 × 5 cup-point set screws. Measure the actual shaft and bushings first. The nominal bushing counterbores are 6.10 mm and may need cleaning or retaining adhesive. Use water-resistant, plastic-compatible grease and a waterproof adhesive compatible with the printed material. Keep grease off bonding surfaces.

## Assembly

1. Finish the holes before assembly. Tap the bridge posts M5 and the radial crank, gear and propeller bosses M3. Keep the shaft and bushing running surfaces smooth; file small flats only under the two shaft set screws.
2. Slip the tube through the molded stern socket. Its 5.6 mm internal passage leaves a grease reservoir around the 4 mm rod. Dry-fit both bushings against their internal stops and insert the rod. Fit the driven gear inside the boat, teeth toward the crank, then fit the propeller behind the boat with its long set-screw boss pointing aft.
3. Install the crank spindle through the underside of the bridge. Fit the keyed handle on top, then screw the bridge to the posts. Rotate to engage the gears. Position the tube so the shaft gear hub and propeller hub each have about 0.3 mm clearance to their adjacent bushing at the design position. Turn the crank through a full revolution and adjust before bonding. Tack the tube to the socket, remove the gears and rod, seal the entire tube-to-hull joint and let the adhesive cure. Seal the bushing outer diameters if they are slip fits.
4. Pack grease into the tube from one open end before seating the second bushing. Insert the shaft slowly while excess grease can escape, wipe off excess, then reinstall the gear and propeller. To service it, loosen the hubs and withdraw the rod. Both bushing bores are running clearances; grease slows leakage and does not establish a watertight seal.
5. First test over a tray, then in shallow water while holding the hull. Check for seepage around the rod and tube socket at rest and while turning. Confirm freeboard and stern trim before letting the boat float free.

The CAD hull-interference screen sampled both rotating assemblies at thirteen crank angles. The printed fits, friction, leakage rate and water performance are unverified.

## Printed PLA bushings

For the hand-cranked prototype, print the separate pla-bushings.stl pair upright. Each sleeve is 6.0 mm OD × 6.0 mm long with a 4.30 mm modeled bore and a short entry chamfer. This gives 0.15 mm nominal radial shaft clearance. Try both on the actual rod, then insert them chamfer-first into the tube and repeat the spin test with both seated. If the shaft binds, lightly finish the bores by hand before adding grease. Check for seepage during the first shallow-water run and replace the sleeves if play develops.
