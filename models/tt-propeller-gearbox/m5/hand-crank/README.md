# Hand crank for the M5 gearbox

A top-mounted crank for the M5 TT propeller gearbox (`../gearbox.coscad`), which printed and ran well on 24 November. The gearbox, gears, propeller, spacers and collar are reused unchanged. The TT motor and its bridge come off. One crank turn turns the propeller once (the five-gear train is 1:1). Generated by `sol build.sol`, which reads the gearbox's own definitions so the cover, socket and bearing sizes stay those of the tested part.

## Parts

`plate.stl` holds four new parts: the replacement **cover**, the **crank**, the handle **sleeve** and the **knob**. `plate-stand.stl` holds the fifth, the **stand**.

- **Cover:** the original cover with one more hole, a 4.3 mm radius bearing over the input gear. It is the same size as the hole the output journal already runs in.
- **Crank:** 32 mm throw. A 4.0 mm radius journal runs in the new cover hole. A 6 mm radius collar lifts the 6 mm arm clear of the cover screws. A stub with the TT motor shaft's double-D profile drops into the input gear's existing socket from above. It is 5.3 mm across and 3.6 mm across the flats, 0.1 mm under the TT shaft that already fits that socket.
- **Stand:** a 10 mm open frame under the floor. With the motor gone, the input gear's journal hangs 1.1 mm below the floor, so the box would rock on it. The stand has a clearance hole for the journal, hex pockets that hold the four cover nuts, and room below them for the M5 × 20 screw tips. It prints flat, about 34 g.
- **Handle:** the knob spins on a flanged printed sleeve. One M5 × 25 screw goes down through the sleeve and the arm into an M5 nut captive in the arm's underside, clamping the sleeve, not the knob.

Print the plate flat as laid out: cover flat, crank arm down with the stub up, sleeve flange down, knob upright. Use the gearbox's usual PLA settings.

## Assembly

Hardware: 4 × M5 × 20 and 1 × M5 × 25 socket-head screws, 5 × M5 hex nuts.

1. Remove the TT motor, its bridge, the old cover and the old cover screws. Leave the gears in place.
2. Press four M5 nuts into the stand's hex pockets and one into the pocket under the crank arm's handle end.
3. Set the gearbox on the stand, input journal over the clearance hole.
4. Fit the new cover. Drive all four M5 × 20 screws from the top, heads up, into the nuts in the stand. A screw from below would stick about 8.5 mm out of the cover, into the crank's path. Finger tight plus a quarter turn.
5. Pass the M5 × 25 screw through the sleeve flange, the sleeve and the knob, then into the crank's nut. Tighten until the sleeve is clamped and the knob still spins freely. This can be done before or after step 6.
6. Turn the input gear until its socket flats line up with the crank stub, then drop the crank through the cover hole until the journal sits in it. Turn the crank by hand through several revolutions before running the propeller in water.

The crank is held down only by gravity and your hand. It lifts straight out, so keep a little downward pressure while cranking.

## Checks

- `coscad check hand-crank.assemble` with the base, input gear, new cover, crank, sleeve and knob in place finds no overlaps. Clearances: stub to socket 0.1 mm, journal to cover hole 0.3 mm, sleeve to knob 0.2 mm.
- `coscad gears crank-sweep.json` turns the crank through 360° in 5° steps past 5 mm socket-cap heads on the two input-end cover screws. The nearest approach is 2.5 mm. The handle screw's tip ends 2 mm below the arm, outside the circle of cover-screw heads.
- `coscad plan hand-crank.assemble` produces a 7-step build with the gearbox flat on the bench and no flips. It loads the nuts first and tightens the cover screws at the cover step. The bill of materials covers all the screws and nuts. The plan is on the manual site under Builds, as hand-crank.
- The gear train itself is the physically tested one and was not re-checked.

At 1:1, a comfortable hand rate of about 2 turns per second gives about 120 rpm at the propeller. The TT-propeller analysis already found that too slow for useful thrust, so expect the propeller to turn but push little. Speeding up would need a different gear pair at the input. The five equal gears sit on fixed 33.2 mm centres, so a step-up means a compound first idler, not just a bigger crank gear. Untested: printed fit of the stub and journal, knob feel, and crank retention in use.
