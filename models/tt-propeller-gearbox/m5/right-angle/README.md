# End-mounted propeller conversion

Print the three parts on the plate. Reuse the freestanding case, lid, input gear, three idlers, propeller spacers and M5 split collar. The new pair uses equal 16-tooth straight miter gears with tapered trapezoidal teeth. It is a nominal 1:1 prototype. The propeller now turns about the gearbox's longitudinal axis.

1. Lift the lid and replace the previous output gear with the compound gear, spur down on the original short pin. Refit the lid around the compound gear's journal.
2. Feed the new propeller shaft through the bracket from the gear side. Bring the bracket onto the two output-end lid holes, turning the gears so a tooth enters a space. Secure the bracket and lid together. Use two M5 × 25 mm screws and the existing nuts here for the extra 3.4 mm of bracket thickness.
3. Install the propeller outside the bearing against the shaft shoulder. Refit the original spacer combination and grooved split collar. The propeller hub and the rear of the driven gear limit shaft endplay. Check free rotation with the propeller retained.
4. Turn by hand through several full revolutions before a low-speed trial. Check for a tight spot, endplay and gear skipping. The miter tooth form is approximate; running torque, wear and thrust retention have not been measured.

Nominal checks used the full standing housing, lid, new bracket, moving pair and a 60 mm × 19 mm propeller envelope. At 23 sampled phases (0 to 22 degrees of the 22.5-degree tooth period) the native mesh intersections were empty. A deliberately mistimed driven gear produced flank interference at eight sampled positions, confirming engagement. The propeller axis is 40 mm above the bench, giving the 60 mm propeller 10 mm nominal tip clearance.
