# TT motor propeller gearbox

Five equal 22-tooth gears put the propeller axis 132.8 mm from the motor axis. Four external meshes preserve the 1:1 speed ratio and direction when viewed from the same side. The shafts are parallel, with the motor and propeller on opposite faces. The 174 × 44 mm case has an additional motor mounting tongue. This is a dry-running prototype; there are no shaft seals.

1. Insert the input gear journal down through the base. Slide the TT motor shaft into its double-D socket from outside. Orient the case over the tongue and secure it with narrow cable ties through the strap slots. Check that the journal turns without rubbing the motor case. The mount assumes the linked yellow TT motor; external housing and lead clearance still need a physical fit check.
2. Drop one idler onto each of the three posts, then seat the output gear on the short end pin with its double-D shaft pointing outward. Turn the gears together to mesh them. Press the cover onto the four pegs and check free rotation by hand.
3. Slide a previously printed propeller onto the output shoulder. Add 2 mm of spacer for P2-50, 5 mm for P3-50, none for P3-60, or both spacers for P4-40. Fit the printed end washer and an M2 × 6 mm thread-forming screw suitable for plastic into the axial pilot. The flats transmit torque; the screw keeps the propeller from walking off.
4. For the initial powered test, run the gearbox without the propeller first and check for binding. Fit, running current, output speed and thrust are unmeasured.

The native mesh check found no interference between adjacent gears at nine sampled phases or between the installed gears and the closed housing at the checked assembly phase. These are nominal geometry checks. The plate includes the housing, lid, five gears, washer and two spacers.
