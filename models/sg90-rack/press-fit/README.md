# Press-fit SG90 drive

Revision 3 (base only; rack, pinion and keeper are unchanged from revision 2). Generated by `sol build.sol`, which writes `plate.coscad`.
Render it with the coscad binary built from this repo (`cabal list-bin coscad`):
the older `bin/coscad` and Homebrew 1.4 builds reject `poly`.

Revision 3 (2026-11-21), after the revision 2 print:

- The 4.95 mm pinion bore press-fits the shaft well. Keep it.
- The SG90's cable leaves from the same end as the output shaft. Revision 2
  had the cable opening in the far wall, so the servo went in backwards and
  the shaft sat about 10 mm too far from the rack. The opening is now a
  full-height slot in the wall facing the rack shelf. The socket already put
  the shaft under the gear, so nothing else moved.
- The socket floor is 3 mm higher (3.8 mm), which lifts the pinion 3 mm to
  meet the rack. The walls and ribs went up with it.
- `build.sol` also writes `base.coscad`, so the base can be reprinted alone.

Changes from revision 1 (printed 2026-11-20). In that print the bore was too
tight and the rack sat off the pinion:

- The pinion has 22 teeth at module 1.5, so the pitch radius is 16.5 mm
  (outside diameter 36 mm). Revision 1 had 20 teeth at module 1, 10 mm.
  The bigger teeth tolerate FDM error much better, and the rack now moves
  about 0.29 mm per degree.
- The bore is 4.95 mm nominal with 0.4 mm lead-in chamfers at both ends.
  Revision 1 had a plain 4.70 mm hole, which printed too small.
- The rack pitch line sits 0.2 mm further from the shaft. The mesh was
  checked over a full tooth pitch and ±25° with no interference, and has
  about 0.2 mm of backlash each way.
- Crush ribs on both end walls of the socket stop the servo sliding toward
  or away from the rack. Revision 1 gripped only the side walls, which
  left 0.9 mm of play along the line that sets the centre distance.
- The shelf front is 1 mm outside the gear tips, so it clears the servo
  ears. Revision 1's shelf started 11 mm from the shaft, right where the
  ears end.
- The pinion is 7 mm thick against a 4 mm rack, so the press depth has
  about 1.5 mm of slack up or down.

## Bore test strip

The strip has five holes, 4.80, 4.90, 5.00, 5.10 and 5.20 mm. The notches
on the edge give the size: one notch is 4.80 and five notches are 5.20.
Try each hole on the bare servo shaft. The right one pushes on firmly by
hand and does not turn. If 4.90 or 5.00 fits better than the pinion's
4.95, change `pinionBore` in `build.sol` and print only the pinion again.

## Assembly

Drop the servo in with its shaft end (the cable end) toward the tall rack
shelf, with the cable through the slot in that wall. Press it down onto the
floor so it seats between the ribs. The cable comes out into the gap between
the socket and the shelf and leaves sideways.

Center the servo electrically and disconnect power. Press the pinion
straight onto the bare shaft by hand. Leave a visible gap above the
stationary turret. Its teeth should cover the rack's full 4 mm height:
the rack runs 28.2 to 32.2 mm above the bed.

Drop the rack slot over the rail with the teeth facing the pinion. Align
the middle rack tooth with a gap on the gear. Press the keeper onto the
two pins until it seats on the rail. Nominal rack clearance is 0.25 mm on
each side of the rail and 0.35 mm below the keeper.

The 47 mm closed slot and 22 mm rail give 25 mm of total mechanical
travel, which is about ±43° on the 16.5 mm pinion. Start with a centered
±25° command (about 14 mm in total). Increase it only after checking both
ends. Keep powered motion short of the hard stops.

## Revision 2 print (2026-11-21)

`x1c-r2/` holds the X1C package: Bambu PLA Basic profile on external PLA,
textured PEI at 55 C, nozzle 220 C, 0.2 mm layers, 4 walls and 20% gyroid
(revision 1 used 2 walls and 8%). Estimate 54 min, 22.4 g, 175 layers.
`x1c-r2/dispatch.py` uploads it once and records `print-run.json`. It refuses
to run again while that record exists. The X1C needs a clean TLS shutdown
after the FTPS upload; bambu-lan's `ftp.py` skips that for the A1 and gets
426 from the X1C.
