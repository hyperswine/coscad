# Plain tube saddle bench test

1. Seat both wings on the front and rear lands, fit the fasteners snugly, and mark each nut and its mating surface with a witness line. Look for rocking or an initial gap. Weigh the assembly and record the actual CG separately.
2. Invert the glider. Support the tube in a broad padded cradle near the tail mount. Hold it against rotation without squeezing the thin tube. Set a phone or magnifier on a fixed stand facing the two lands, with a millimetre scale in the same plane. Put paired reference dots on the wing root and the adjacent saddle, and another on the nearby tube wall.
3. Place a soft sling about 100 mm outboard of the root, near the main spar. Include the sling in the hanging mass. On one wing, start with 25 g (0.25 N), then 50 g (0.49 N), then 100 g (0.98 N) only if the previous step is reversible. Support the mass while attaching it, then lower it slowly. Hold each step for 10 seconds, photograph both saddles and unload. Repeat on the other side with the same station and fixture.
4. Stop for a click, cracking, whitening, nut rotation, persistent gap, or residual offset beyond the measurement repeatability. Compare the loaded and unloaded photographs. Wing-to-saddle motion indicates seating or fastener movement. Saddle-to-wall motion points to the saddle or its transition into the tube. Movement of all three marks together suggests tube or fixture flex. Record load, side, front/rear observations and residual motion. This is a prototype comparison, not a proof load. The outboard sling introduces a moment absent from the direct-pad FEA, so do not use the simulated displacement as a pass limit.
